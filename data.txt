## Sam Altman (@sama)

Sam Altman called for a new industry-wide standard to govern frontier AI development, centered on **perplexity as a shared metric**.
Sam Altman 呼吁建立新的行业标准来约束前沿 AI 研发，核心是以**困惑度（perplexity）作为共同度量指标**。

He proposed that labs jointly commit to concrete development limits—measured by perplexity and other metrics—until AI systems can be properly understood and monitored, such as through successor CoT monitors.
他提出，各家实验室应共同承诺设定明确的开发上限——以困惑度等指标衡量——直到 AI 系统能被充分理解和监控，例如通过升级版思维链（CoT）监控机制。

Under the White House Accords, labs have already agreed to establish safety standards; Altman sees the perplexity discussion as a natural extension of those commitments.
根据白宫协议，各实验室已同意制定安全标准；Altman 认为围绕困惑度展开的讨论是这些承诺的自然延伸。

## 🎙 Podcast: AI & I by Every — "A New Vision for Human Work After Automation"

**Published:** 2026-11-11 | **Source:** Every.to | **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WstNofgQF5Q

### 一、深度总结

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, delivered the closing talk at Every's first conference (V.C.S.S.) in Brooklyn, tackling one central question: *What does great human work look like after automation?*

He contrasts two methods of predicting the future—the **aggregate** (trend lines, benchmarks) and the **intimate** (zooming in on one real case). OpenAI's GDP Val benchmark shows AI beating human experts at expert tasks 83% of the time in 2026, up from 12.4% in 2024. This looks terrifying in aggregate.

But Shipper's own week tells a different story: using Claude, he analyzed a customer survey, built a live video game for the all-hands, vibe-coded a custom CRM, and sent personalized outreach—work that would have required five different roles in 2020. This *feels* almost normal now.

The key insight: when the same survey data was handed to the whole team, each person found something different. Yash (growth) found a churn problem caused by too much email. Douglas (marketing) found that readers feel isolated among non-AI-believers. Jack (editor) made a Claymation game show. Claude's default prompt? Generic true-but-cheap answers that everyone already has.

**Generation is cheap. Ranking is hard.** Claude can predict what Shipper would say in Slack 93% of the time—but only if it's given 400 options. It can only rank the right answer in the top 10% about 60% of the time. What's valuable is the perspective that lets you *find* the right answer in the noise.

The takeaway: as model capabilities become distributed and the floor rises for everyone, the **alpha is in your perspective and responsibility**. You don't compete with 2027 Claude—you use 2027 Claude from your unique vantage point. Human work doesn't go to zero; it rides on top of ever-improving model capability.

Shipper calls this the **allocation economy**: "You won't be judged on how much you know, but on how well you can allocate and manage the resources of intelligence."

### 二、核心洞见

这是一个关于"自动化之后，人类工作的价值何在"的深度演讲，来自 Every 的 CEO Dan Shipper。

他从两种预测未来的方式出发：**宏观聚合**（趋势线、基准测试）和**微观亲历**（聚焦于真实案例细节）。从宏观来看，2026 年 AI 在专家任务中击败人类专家的概率已达 83%，令人不安。

但 Shipper 自己上周的经历讲述了截然不同的故事：他用 Claude 分析了客户调查、为全员会议做了一个实时视频游戏、"氛围编程"了一个 CRM 并发送了个性化外联——这些工作在 2020 年需要五种不同角色，而现在感觉几乎稀松平常。

**关键洞见：生成是廉价的，排序才是困难的。** 当同一份调查数据交给整个团队时，每个人都找到了不同的东西——而 Claude 的默认回答只是廉价的通用真相，人人可得。Claude 能以 93% 的准确率预测 Shipper 会在 Slack 上说什么，但前提是给出 400 个选项，且正确答案仅有 60% 的概率进入前 10%。

因此，当模型能力成为所有人的"地板"，真正的**阿尔法收益来自你的视角与责任感**。你不是在与 2027 年的 Claude 竞争——而是用 2027 年的 Claude，从你独特的视角出发去工作。Shipper 将此称为**分配经济（allocation economy）**：未来，你被评判的不是你知道多少，而是你能多好地分配和管理智能资源。
