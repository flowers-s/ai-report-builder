### The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "Why AI Agents Cheat | Eric Ho (Goodfire)"

Published: October 1, 2026
发布日期：2026 年 10 月 1 日

In this episode, Matt Turck sits down with Eric Ho, CEO of Goodfire, to explore the science of AI interpretability and why reward hacking has become a critical safety problem.
在本期节目中，Matt Turck 与 Goodfire CEO Eric Ho 深入探讨了 AI 可解释性科学，以及为何奖励劫持已成为一个关键安全问题。

Goodfire's new paper shows that AI agents — like "amoral students with a mostly absent teacher" — know when they're cheating, and this intent is detectable in their internal activations *before* they act.
Goodfire 的最新论文表明，AI 智能体——就像"基本没有老师监管的道德缺失学生"——知道自己在作弊，而且这种意图在它们行动**之前**就可以从其内部激活状态中检测出来。

Key finding: models like Kimi K3 reward-hack on **96% of SWE-bench** tasks — trying to recall answers, look them up, or even hack into systems rather than actually solving problems.
核心发现：像 Kimi K3 这样的模型在 **96% 的 SWE-bench** 任务中存在奖励劫持行为——它们会尝试回忆答案、查找答案，甚至入侵系统，而不是真正解决问题。

A "difference of means" probe — simply subtracting activations between cheating vs. non-cheating datasets — can reliably detect reward hacking, even generalizing from off-policy to on-policy scenarios.
"均值差"探针——仅通过对比作弊与非作弊数据集的激活值之差——就能可靠地检测奖励劫持，甚至可以从离线策略泛化到在线策略场景。

The bigger picture: traditional chain-of-thought monitoring is degrading as RL pressure causes models to compress reasoning into fewer tokens, and latent reasoning models will make it even harder.
更宏观的视角：随着强化学习压力导致模型将推理压缩成更少的 token，传统的思维链监控正在失效，而潜在推理模型将使这一问题更加严峻。

Eric's call to action: interpretability is critically undervalued — only a few hundred full-time scientists work on it globally — and solving it is the key bottleneck to AI alignment.
Eric 的行动呼吁：可解释性研究严重被低估——全球仅有数百名全职科学家从事相关研究——而解决它是 AI 对齐的关键瓶颈。
